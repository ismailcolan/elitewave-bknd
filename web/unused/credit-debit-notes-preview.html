Credit note and debit note

Real transport billing: you already issued a GST tax invoice (or booked a vendor expense). You cannot rewrite that paper. A note adjusts the remaining To Pay and GST books.

Client formula (receivable) To Pay = Invoice amount − Already paid − Credit notes + Debit notes. Worked: 11,800 − 5,000 − 2,360 = 4,440. Do not subtract To Pay again.

You billed the client too high → Credit note

Invoice is Final. Client disputes freight (rate, weight, extra halt billed by mistake). You issue a credit note against that invoice. Client owes less. Original PDF stays. GST output tax also reduces.

Example: billed ₹10,000 + 18% = ₹11,800. Agreed freight was ₹8,000. CN taxable ₹2,000 + GST ₹360 = ₹2,360.

You billed the client too low → Debit note

Invoice already Final. Extra detention, extra weight, or missed GCN found later. You issue a debit note against the same invoice. Client owes more. GST output tax increases.

Example: extra detention ₹1,000 + 18% = ₹1,180. To Pay goes up by 1,180.

Same story with the vendor (you pay them)

Expense / vendor bill is the “invoice” in the other direction. Who issues the paper flips the name, not the money.

What happenedPaper you recordVendor To Pay
Hire/hamali billed higher than agreed. You debit the vendor. Vendor debit note (you issue) Goes down
Vendor admits overcharge and sends you a credit note. Vendor credit note received Goes down (same effect)
Vendor charges extra after their bill (detention they claim). Vendor debit note received Goes up
Do not mix the two debit notes “You debit the vendor” reduces payable. “Vendor debit note to you” increases payable. Elite Wave first build: client CN, then client DN, then you-issue vendor DN.
